You filed your Certificate of Formation, the Secretary of State approved it, and your new Washington LLC is official. Congratulations. Within a week or two, something else will happen: your mailbox (and maybe your inbox and text messages) will start filling up with "notices" that look urgent, official, and expensive.

Most of them aren't from the government. And most of what they're selling is either free or something you don't need at all.

The reason this happens is simple. When you form an LLC, your business name, address, UBI number, and formation date become public record. Third-party companies pull that data and send mailers designed to look like government bills. The same thing happens again every year around your annual report deadline.

Here's what to expect, what's real, and how B2B Solutions handles it for our clients.

## Right After You Form Your LLC

These usually show up in the first few weeks.

**"Labor law poster" compliance notices.** These warn that you must display federal and state workplace posters and offer to sell you a "compliance kit" for $80 to $300. If you have employees, you do need to post certain notices. But the posters themselves are **free** from Washington L&I and the U.S. Department of Labor. You never need to buy them.

**EIN / tax ID number offers.** Some sites and mailers charge $100 or more to "obtain your federal Employer Identification Number." The IRS issues EINs **for free**, online, in minutes. Anyone charging you for the number itself is charging you for something the IRS gives away.

**"Certificate of Existence" or "Certificate of Good Standing" offers.** These letters make it sound like you're required to order a certificate proving your LLC exists. You aren't. The Secretary of State notes that a certificate is only needed if someone in the course of your business asks for one (a bank or lender, for example). If you do need one, you can order it directly from the Secretary of State for far less than these mailers charge.

**"Annual minutes" or "corporate records" services.** These claim you must file annual meeting minutes with the state, often for $150 or more. Washington does **not** require LLCs to file meeting minutes with the state.

**Trademark and "business registry" listings.** Letters that look like they come from a federal trademark office, or that offer to list your business in an official-sounding "registry." These are private companies. Registering your LLC with the state does not require any of them.

**Beneficial ownership (BOI) reporting scams.** Under federal rules updated in 2025, U.S.-formed companies are no longer required to file beneficial ownership reports with FinCEN. Mailers and emails demanding a BOI filing fee from a Washington LLC should be treated with suspicion.

## Around Your Annual Report Deadline

Every Washington LLC must file an annual report with the Secretary of State by the end of its anniversary month. That predictable deadline is exactly what makes it a target.

You'll likely see letters, emails, or text messages with names like "Annual Report Compliance," "Washington Annual Reports," or "State of Washington Business Entities." Some use the state seal. Some include your real UBI number and expiration date. Some threaten late fees or dissolution if you don't pay right away.

These companies typically charge $150 to $250 or more to file a report the state charges a fraction of that for. In recent years the Washington Attorney General has taken legal action against companies that sent hundreds of thousands of these deceptive messages to Washington businesses.

**How to tell a real Secretary of State notice from a solicitation:**

- **The Secretary of State does not send text messages** about annual reports. Any text is not from the state.
- **Real notices come on a predictable schedule.** The state sends your annual report reminder around the first of the month before your anniversary month. A "notice" that arrives at a random time is a red flag.
- **Look for the fine print.** Many solicitations include a small disclaimer saying they are "not a government agency" or "not affiliated with the State of Washington."
- **Check the payment address.** Official payments go through the Secretary of State's Corporations and Charities Filing System (CCFS), not a private company or P.O. box.
- **When in doubt, look yourself up.** You can check your LLC's status and annual report due date directly on [CCFS](https://ccfs.sos.wa.gov/). The Secretary of State also keeps a page of [examples of misleading notices and solicitations](https://www.sos.wa.gov/corporations-charities/frequently-asked-questions-faqs/third-party-notices-and-solicitations-tips-examples).
