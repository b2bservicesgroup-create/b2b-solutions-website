Starting a business is exciting. You've got your EIN, a business bank account, and maybe your first few customers. Then the real work begins: keeping the business side running while you do the thing you actually started the company to do.

After years of helping small business owners with bookkeeping, payroll, and the behind-the-scenes work, we see the same struggles come up again and again. The good news is that every one of them has a simple fix. Here's what trips people up, and what you need to get past it.

## 1. Mixing Personal and Business Money

**The struggle:** A fuel fill-up here, a business lunch on the personal card there. Before long, nobody can tell what the business really earned or spent.

**What you need:** A dedicated business account and card, used for everything business-related, and nothing else. Decide how you'll pay yourself (owner draws or payroll) and stick to it. Clean lines now save hours of untangling later.

## 2. Not Knowing What You Owe

**The struggle:** Tax time arrives and so does the surprise bill, because nothing was set aside along the way.

**What you need:** A simple habit of moving a percentage of every deposit into a separate savings account for taxes. Learn which filings apply to your business, such as estimated tax payments, state excise tax, and sales tax. Late or missed filings can cost you penalties, even when you owe nothing.

## 3. Letting the Bookkeeping Pile Up

**The struggle:** Receipts get lost, transactions go uncategorized, and "I'll catch up this weekend" turns into a year-end cleanup project.

**What you need:** A regular routine, weekly or monthly, and bookkeeping software that fits your business. Even 30 minutes of consistent attention beats a month of catching up. If it's not getting done, that's a sign it's time to hand it off.

## 4. Contractor or Employee? Not Sure

**The struggle:** You bring on some help and assume they're a contractor. But how the work is done, not what you call it, determines the classification. Getting it wrong can mean back taxes and penalties.

**What you need:** A W-9 from every contractor, a written agreement, and a clear understanding of when someone should really be an employee. When in doubt, ask before the first payment goes out. (See our guide to [independent contractor vs. employee in Washington](/blog/independent-contractor-vs-employee-washington).)

## 5. Payroll and Compliance Surprises

**The struggle:** Hiring your first employee brings registrations, withholding, filings, and new-hire paperwork you didn't know existed.

**What you need:** Payroll set up correctly from day one, with employer registrations in place and someone making sure the filings go out on time. (Start with [hiring your first employee in Washington State](/blog/hiring-your-first-employee-in-washington-state).)

## 6. Not Reading Your Own Numbers

**The struggle:** You have financial reports, but they might as well be written in another language. So decisions get made on gut feel and the bank balance.

**What you need:** A monthly look at your profit and loss and your cash flow, explained in plain English. Once you know what your numbers say, you can price better, spend smarter, and plan ahead.

## 7. Trying to Do It All Yourself

**The struggle:** Admin work quietly eats the hours you should be spending on customers and growth.

**What you need:** To know when to delegate. Bookkeeping, HR and payroll, and administrative support are all things you can hand off, often for less than the value of your time.
