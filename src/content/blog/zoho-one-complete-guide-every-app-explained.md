Ask ten small business owners what Zoho One is and you will get ten different answers. Some know it as the CRM. Some know it as the email. A few know it as the thing their bookkeeper keeps raving about. Almost nobody knows the whole picture, because the whole picture is big.

Zoho One is a single subscription that bundles more than 45 apps (Zoho itself describes the suite as 45+ on some pages and 50+ on others, and the list keeps growing) under one login, one admin console, and one invoice. We run B2B Solutions on it, and we build it out for clients every week. This guide walks through the apps by what they do for your business, so you can see what is in the box and which pieces are worth turning on first.

If you want the shorter, story-driven version, start with [What Can Zoho One Actually Do for a Small Business?](/blog/what-can-zoho-one-do-for-a-small-business/). This post is the reference list.

A quick note before we dive in: Zoho adds and renames apps regularly, and some apps are available in different editions or regions. Treat this as a map, and check Zoho's current app list or ask us before you plan around any single feature.

## How to read this list

You do not need all of it. Most small businesses will actively use somewhere between five and fifteen apps. The rest sit quietly in the background until the day you need them, and on that day, they are already paid for, already connected, and already logged in. That is the real value of the suite.

## Sales and customer relationships

**Zoho CRM.** The center of the platform. It tracks leads, contacts, companies, deals, and every email, call, and task tied to them. We build custom pipelines in CRM for clients all the time, from intake to proposal to onboarding. It is also the app most other Zoho apps connect to first.

**Zoho Bookings.** Online appointment scheduling. Clients pick a time from your real availability, get reminders, and the booking lands in your calendar and CRM. We use it for consultations.

**Zoho SalesIQ.** Website visitor tracking and live chat, so you can see who is on your site and talk to them while they are there.

## Marketing and social media

**Zoho Campaigns.** Email marketing: newsletters, drip sequences, and segmented lists, tied back to your CRM so you know who opened what.

**Zoho Social.** Schedule and publish posts to Facebook, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile and more from one calendar. This very blog's promotion runs through it.

**Zoho Marketing Automation.** Multi-step customer journeys that react to behavior, such as a visitor downloading a guide and then getting a follow-up sequence.

**Zoho Forms.** Build web forms for leads, intake, surveys, and requests, with submissions flowing straight into your other apps.

**Zoho Thrive.** Tools for referral, advocacy, and loyalty programs, for businesses that want customers recruiting customers.

**Zoho PageSense.** Website analytics and conversion tools, including A/B testing, so you can learn which page version actually gets responses.

## Websites and online selling

**Zoho Sites.** A website builder for business sites and landing pages.

**Zoho Commerce.** An online store builder for selling products, with payments and inventory connections.

## Finance and accounting

**Zoho Books.** Full online accounting: invoices, bills, bank feeds, reconciliation, reports, and tax-ready records. This is the app that surprises people most, because it holds up as a real bookkeeping platform. We use it for our own books.

**Zoho Invoice.** Straightforward invoicing for people who do not need full accounting.

**Zoho Expense.** Receipt capture and expense reports, so employees stop handing you crumpled paper.

**Zoho Billing.** Recurring billing and subscription management for businesses with retainers or memberships.

**Zoho Checkout.** Payment pages you can send to customers without building a website.

**Zoho Inventory.** Stock tracking, orders, and warehouses for product businesses.

## People and HR

**Zoho People.** Employee records, time off, attendance, timesheets, and onboarding checklists. It is the backbone of a lightweight HR system.

**Zoho Recruit.** Job postings, applicant tracking, and hiring pipelines, for the day you stop being able to find people by word of mouth.

## Customer support

**Zoho Desk.** Help desk and ticketing. Customer emails, calls, and chats become tracked tickets with owners and deadlines.

**Zoho Assist.** Remote support and screen sharing, to help a customer or employee from wherever you are.

**Zoho Lens.** Augmented reality support sessions, where a customer points their phone camera and you guide them live.

## Projects and operations

**Zoho Projects.** Tasks, milestones, timelines, and time tracking for client work and internal initiatives.

**Zoho Sprints.** Agile project management, mostly for software and product teams.

## Email, chat, and collaboration

**Zoho Mail.** Business email on your own domain, with calendar, contacts, and tasks built in. A clean replacement for the old inbox-per-tool habit.

**Zoho Cliq.** Team chat with channels, direct messages, and video calls.

**Zoho Meeting.** Webinars and online meetings.

**Zoho Connect.** An internal social network and announcement board for teams that want one place for company news and discussion.

**Zoho TeamInbox.** A shared inbox for the addresses everyone watches, like info@ or support@, with assignments so nothing gets answered twice or not at all.

**Zoho Notebook.** Notes, checklists, and clipped web content.

**Zoho Vani.** Zoho's voice and telephony service, for calling and call tracking connected to your apps.

## Documents, files, and knowledge

**Zoho WorkDrive.** Cloud file storage and team folders, with sharing controls and a place for every client's documents. We use it as our central filing cabinet.

**Zoho Writer.** Word processing with real-time collaboration, mail merge, and document templates that pull data from your CRM.

**Zoho Sheet.** Spreadsheets with collaboration built in.

**Zoho Show.** Presentations.

**Zoho Sign.** Legally binding e-signatures. Proposals, agreements, and onboarding documents get signed online and filed automatically. We use it every week.

**Zoho Learn.** A knowledge base and training platform. We keep our team procedures here so answers live in one searchable place instead of in someone's head.

## Automation, custom apps, and integration

This is the layer that turns a pile of apps into a system.

**Zoho Flow.** Connects apps to each other and to outside tools, with triggers and actions. When a form comes in, create the CRM record, send the email, and notify the team, without anyone retyping a thing.

**Zoho Creator.** A low-code builder for custom apps. When no off-the-shelf software fits your process, you build the app you actually need. Our client onboarding runs on custom Creator apps.

**Zoho RPA.** Robotic process automation for repetitive, click-heavy tasks across apps that do not otherwise talk to each other.

**Custom functions.** Small scripts that extend Zoho apps when a built-in setting does not quite do the job.

## Analytics, AI, and admin tools

**Zoho Analytics.** Dashboards and reports that pull data from across your apps, so you can see sales, spending, and workload in one place.

**Zia.** Zoho's AI assistant, woven through the apps for answers, predictions, and suggestions.

**Zia Search.** One search box across your Zoho data.

**User provisioning and security.** Add or remove a person in one place and their access across every app follows. Set security policies once for the whole organization. When an employee leaves, this is the feature you will be glad you set up.

## What is not in the box

Zoho One does not include absolutely everything Zoho makes, and some apps are included at a particular edition rather than the top tier. A few specialty products and certain advanced features are sold separately. That is exactly why we recommend a short planning conversation before you commit to any build.

## Where to start

Having 45-plus apps available does not mean you should switch them all on at once. That is the fastest way to end up with an expensive pile of half-configured software. Here is the order we usually suggest:

1. **Start with the foundation.** Mail, WorkDrive, and CRM, so people, files, and contacts live in one place.
2. **Add money.** Books, plus Expense or Invoice if they fit, so your accounting stops being a separate world.
3. **Add the paper trail.** Sign and Forms, so agreements and intake stop being email attachments.
4. **Connect it.** Flow ties it together so the busywork disappears.
5. **Customize last.** Creator, Writer templates, and Analytics are most powerful once you know exactly what your process looks like.
